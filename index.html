Papers

2026, manuscript

Papertrend: An evidence-grounded platform for reading, charting, and questioning a research collection with large language models

Pheemaphat Chantarusorn, Thanawat Traipat, Jakapun Tachaiya

A web platform that reads a program's theses with a LangGraph pipeline, stores a sentence of evidence for every keyword it extracts, and reports a shift in the field only when it survives the removal of any single paper.

Open the platform

Graduate programs accumulate research faster than anyone can read it as a body. The English as an International Language (EIL) program at Chulalongkorn University records 182 master's theses and doctoral dissertations from 2006 to 2025, of which 101 survive as retrievable PDF files. An earlier study of that record ran a one-off chain of language-model agents in a notebook. This paper presents Papertrend, the web platform that grew out of it.

Papertrend reads each uploaded paper with a LangGraph pipeline that stores a sentence of evidence for every keyword it extracts and a quoted line for every publication year it assigns. It groups topics across papers by the consensus of three independent model groupings, and reports a shift in the field only when the shift survives the removal of any one paper. It answers questions over the collection with four-channel hybrid retrieval, whole-paper reading and passage-level citations, and it audits a draft whose support is in doubt. A table view extracts reader-defined fields from every paper and keeps a value only when its quotation, and any number in it, occurs in the paper.

On a 14-paper gold set that includes scanned and Thai papers, a pipeline revision raised correct publication years from 21% to 100% and keywords found verbatim in the text from 98% to 100%, at a median cost of US$0.0085 per paper. On a development sample of 41 uploaded EIL papers, 45 of 48 benchmark questions received right, specific and cited answers.

2026, capstone report, in Thai

A multi-agent question-answering prototype for organizational knowledge under knowledge governance

การพัฒนาต้นแบบระบบถาม-ตอบความรู้องค์กรด้วยเอเจนต์หลายตัวภายใต้กลไกธรรมาภิบาลความรู้

Pheemaphat Chantarusorn. Advised by Assoc. Prof. Dr. Songphan Choemprayong

Asks what governance a system needs before its answers from an organisation's own documents can be trusted and traced. The prototype carries knowledge through six stages, from intake and human verification to cited answers, and logs every question it cannot answer as a knowledge gap that sets what to collect next.

Built during an internship at an insurance company, the prototype turned 19 of 22 permitted internal documents into 1,597 published knowledge items across six knowledge packs. Five experiments found no citation leaking from one pack into another and a citation accuracy of 1.000.

Classifying the intent of a question proved to be a governance mechanism rather than a convenience: it raised scope containment from 0.524 to 1.000 without lowering retrieval coverage. Because Thai is written without spaces, character n-gram retrieval almost always finds some match, so "no documents found" cannot signal that a question is out of scope. The report also separates an owner who is declared from an owner who has actually taken responsibility, applying the evidence-control principles of library science to an AI system.
